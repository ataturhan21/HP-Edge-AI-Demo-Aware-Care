EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
Local AI Health Guidance Platform
Executive summary
This product demonstrates how a healthcare organization can guide a patient from a health concern to an appropriate next step using AI on one local HP ZGX device. It combines an adaptive consultation, relevant records, practical guidance and explained doctor matches in one responsive website and mobile app experience.
For healthcare CIOs and CTOs, the value proposition is better care navigation, less avoidable clinician workload, better prepared appointments and potential reductions in unnecessary episode spending. These are outcomes to validate. The demo proves the workflow and local execution; it does not prove clinical effectiveness or financial savings.
How to use this master document
Read pages 1 to 5 for executive value, pages 6 to 16 for the complete experience and pages 17 onward for implementation. The implementation section defines stable requirements, contracts, code ownership and tests. Version 2.0 replaces the earlier planning document. All code paths are proposed until implemented and verified; no application code is claimed to exist.
EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
Where healthcare value comes from
The product helps users choose the right level of care and prepare useful information for that encounter. It can reduce avoidable demand only when guidance is appropriate, understood and followed without delaying necessary evaluation.
Less burden with better access
A concise intake and portable summary can reduce repetitive questioning. Relevant specialty matching can reduce misdirected appointments and referral rework. Reviewed advice may resolve suitable low-acuity questions without an unnecessary visit. Urgent concerns must move toward timely evaluation rather than being optimized for fewer encounters.
Who can realize the benefit
Patients may save time, travel and out-of-pocket expense. Clinicians may recover capacity for complex care. Health systems may reduce scheduling friction. Insurers and organizations accountable for total cost may benefit from more appropriate utilization. Under fee-for-service arrangements, fewer visits can reduce revenue; capacity gains and payer savings are different economic outcomes [S1, S2].

B01  Patients | Better choice of doctor. Specialty guidance and preference matching can reduce wrong-destination appointments. | Appropriate-first-destination rate; repeat referrals
B02  Clinicians | Less avoidable demand. Reviewed advice for suitable concerns may resolve questions without an unnecessary visit. | Clinically adjudicated avoidable visits; delayed-care events
B03  Clinical staff | Less repetitive intake. Confirmed histories and concise summaries can reduce repeat questioning and preparation time. | Net staff minutes per episode, including correction time
B04  Health systems | More useful appointment capacity. Redirecting unsuitable demand can free slots for patients needing those services. | Wait time and filled capacity by specialty
B05  Insurers and risk-bearing providers | Lower avoidable episode spending. Fewer inappropriate visits and better navigation may lower total episode cost. | Risk-adjusted allowed cost per episode with quality controls
B06  Patients and caregivers | Less travel and confusion. Clear actions, visit options and a portable summary can reduce navigation effort. | Time to next step; out-of-pocket cost; comprehension
B07  CIO and privacy teams | Greater control of sensitive data. Local inference and lifecycle controls reduce dependence on external processing. | Verified outbound payloads; deletion completion; access tests
B08  CTO and operations teams | Observable and adaptable AI delivery. Versioned local models, visible evidence and reproducible builds support technical oversight. | Reproducible run rate; recovery time; measured cost per run
Additional potential effects include better health literacy, greater patient confidence, less navigation work for caregivers and more consistent intake. They depend on usability, source quality and appropriate follow-through. Clinical staff must not inherit a larger correction or escalation workload than the platform removes [S3].
EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
How to validate value and spending
Measure appropriate care rather than visit avoidance alone
Use a prospective pilot with a matched comparison group or randomized rollout where feasible. Define eligible episodes, baseline utilization, follow-up windows and exclusions before analysis. A clinician reviewer should adjudicate potentially avoidable visits and inappropriate delays using a blinded process where practical. Patient intent to skip a visit is not a verified avoided visit.
Outcome | Definition and evidence
Appropriate destination | Among reviewed episodes that need clinical care, the share whose first destination matches the adjudicated care setting or specialty. Report access failures separately.
Net clinical time | Baseline intake, navigation and documentation time minus the pilot time, including AI-output review, correction, escalation and support.
Episode spending | Total allowed medical cost for eligible episodes over a predefined window, adjusted for case mix and compared with a valid baseline. Report patient out-of-pocket cost separately.
Quality and access | Urgency disagreements, delayed necessary care, repeat contacts, escalation events, time to care and user comprehension. Report differences across relevant groups.
An honest economic model
Payer net value = attributable reduction in total allowed episode cost minus program operating cost. Provider net value = usable capacity or labor benefit plus applicable quality or risk-contract benefit minus operating cost and any lost contribution margin. Do not count the same episode reduction twice, and do not call freed minutes cash savings unless expenditure actually falls.
Local delivery cost includes device amortization, energy, support, model and source licensing, maintenance, clinical review and integration. Compare equivalent workload, quality and utilization assumptions before claiming that local inference costs less than a cloud alternative. This document assigns no invented savings percentage or return on investment.
What the demo can measure now
Completion, time per step, response latency, valid citations, directory filter accuracy and network behavior are measurable with synthetic cases. Real referral outcomes, avoided visits, insurer savings and patient health outcomes remain “Not measured” until a suitable pilot supplies evidence [S1, S2].
EXECUTIVE OVERVIEW
Local execution and executive proof
One ZGX device hosts the application, local AI inference, health data, document processing and evidence stores. The retrieval gateway is also local; only approved requests cross to external public sources. The browser runs on a demonstration computer connected through a trusted network [S4].
A five minute presentation
0:00 to 1:00: explain the concern and local data choices. 1:00 to 2:00: confirm a report fact and answer two adaptive questions, with earlier answers transparently prefilled. 2:00 to 3:00: show guidance and its supporting source. 3:00 to 4:00: compare doctor matches and preview the summary. 4:00 to 5:00: show actual runtime measurements, cached operation and guest reset.
Use a clearly fictional patient. Prepare a separate urgent-interruption example. The healthcare outcome story is a hypothesis; the live proof is the complete workflow, controlled processing and honest handling of uncertainty.

IMPLEMENTATION SPECIFICATION
The document to code contract
This master document is the product authority for version 2.0. The AI must implement the numbered contracts, not improvise behavior from the executive narrative. Stable identifiers create traceability; executable checks and review catch drift. No document can guarantee that an AI will always interpret every instruction correctly.
Authority and synchronization
Requirements R01 to R40 are normative. Screen contracts govern routes and user behavior; object and API contracts govern data interchange; module contracts govern implementation ownership. Benefits are hypotheses and cannot override safety or acceptance rules. When two normative clauses conflict, record the conflict and obtain a product decision rather than silently choosing one.
At repository bootstrap, copy this baseline into spec/product.md and preserve all IDs. Maintain spec/traceability.yaml, spec/screens.yaml and spec/contracts/*.schema.json from the same approved baseline. Record a common spec version and SHA-256 of normalized spec/product.md in spec/lock.json. Every approved change updates the source specification, document export, generated contracts and lock in one pull request. An edited Word file must be reconciled before implementation resumes.

Core data contracts
Contract version 2.0 uses UUID identifiers, UTF-8 strings and UTC ISO 8601 timestamps. Store unknown explicitly. Distinguish absent optional fields from explicit negative findings. All writes are validated server-side; reject unknown fields. Database migrations define foreign keys, uniqueness constraints and deletion cascades.
Object | Required fields and constraints
Session | id, actor_id, mode={guest,saved}, role={patient,presenter}, consent_version, consent_at, adult_eligible, created_at, last_seen_at, expires_at. Role is assigned by authentication, never client input.
Profile | id, actor_id, revision>=1, age_years, history[], medications[], allergies, saved_at. Allergy status={unknown,none_reported,reported}; each reported item has value and confirmation timestamp.
Case | id, actor_id, revision>=1, state, concern, confirmed_context[], issued_slots=0..10, reviewed_revision nullable, latest_guidance_id nullable, created_at, updated_at, expires_at, deleted_at nullable.
Fact | id, case_id, kind, value, units nullable, provenance={user,profile,document}, source_id nullable, page nullable, status={unconfirmed,confirmed,rejected}, original_text nullable, confirmed_at nullable.
Question | id, case_id, slot=1..10, template_id, pathway_version, issued_revision, response_type, text, choices[], required, reason, constraints, status={active,answered,skipped,invalidated}.
Answer | id, case_id, question_id, status={answered,unknown,skipped}, value typed by response_type, revision, submitted_at. Exactly one current answer per question; edits keep minimal revision metadata.
Job | id, actor_id, case_id nullable, input_revision nullable, kind, status={queued,running,succeeded,failed,stale,cancelled}, result_id nullable, error_code nullable, created_at, finished_at nullable.
Concurrency and provenance
Clinical writes increment Case.revision transactionally. reviewed_revision must equal the current revision before generation. A background job commits only when ownership, revision and deletion checks still pass. Provider filter changes create a new search version rather than altering clinical answers. Store source facts separately from AI inferences so a generated statement never silently becomes patient history.

Validation rules
Reject invented IDs and unsupported URLs. Lists may be empty only where the case justifies it, with an explicit uncertainty. Guidance urgency must equal or be more conservative than the reviewed urgency decision according to a defined policy; model prose cannot lower it. A self-care candidate designation requires the relevant pathway’s complete required inputs and approved criteria, not simply the absence of a matched urgent rule.
Evidence quality
Use a reviewed corpus with publisher, provenance, relevance and freshness checks [S5]. The evidence validator checks both source identity and statement support. A valid source identifier alone is not proof of support. For the first demo, restrict clinical guidance to reviewed claim templates or clinician-reviewed fixture outputs; free-form expansion is evaluated before inclusion.

IMPLEMENTATION SPECIFICATION
API conventions and session operations
All endpoints below use /api/v1 as their prefix and expose operation IDs exactly as listed. Generate the TypeScript client from the backend’s exported OpenAPI contract. Do not maintain hand-written duplicate request types. Public informational pages use bundled content rather than separate health-data APIs.
Common request rules
Session creation validates Origin and establishes a CSRF token. Subsequent writes require a secure session, matching Origin and CSRF protection. Health-data writes require current consent. Case mutations carry expected_revision. Retryable creation calls carry Idempotency-Key; scope keys to actor, operation and request hash, retain for 24 hours, and reject key reuse with different payloads. Authorization is checked for every nested ID.
Return errors as {code, message, field_errors, retryable, request_id, current_revision?}. Use 400 for malformed input, 401 for no session, 403 for a role or consent failure, 404 for absent or foreign-owned resources, 409 for stale revision/state, 413 for size, 415 for type, 422 for schema, 429 for queue saturation and 503 for required service failure. Never include raw model output in errors.

IMPLEMENTATION SPECIFICATION
Case state machine and revisions
State | Permitted transition and guard
draft | Concern saved and initial checks passed → intake. Urgent signal → urgent. Unsupported scope or failed required safety service → limited.
intake | Answer, record confirmation or edit → intake after recheck. Required context complete or ten issued slots → review. Urgent signal → urgent.
review | User confirms current revision → reviewed. Any clinical edit → intake with changed facts and unresolved gaps.
reviewed | Generate request with reviewed_revision == revision → generating. Clinical edit → intake.
generating | Valid current output → guided. Invalid output after retry → limited. Edit → intake and mark pending output stale. Urgent result → urgent.
guided | Read guidance, evidence, matches or summary. Clinical edit → intake and invalidates all derived results. New concern creates a new case.
urgent | Only urgent instructions, history and deletion are available. Routine intake, doctor ranking and normal guidance are blocked. Restart requires a new case.
limited | Explain scope, missing context or service failure. Retry only for recoverable failures; corrected supported context may return to intake.
deleting and deleted | Immediately revoke access. Cancel jobs, purge dependencies, then mark deletion complete. No transition back to active.
Revision transaction
Lock the case row; check actor, expected_revision and allowed transition; validate the mutation; increment revision; invalidate derived pointers; run applicable safety checks; persist the new state and a content-free event. A stale write returns 409 with current_revision so the UI can reload and ask the user to reconcile the edit. Do not silently overwrite newer answers.
Question accounting under edits
Allocate a slot only when a new prompt is issued. Re-fetching or retrying the same active question is idempotent. Answering, skipping or revising it does not add a slot. An invalidated prompt still consumes its issued slot, so edits cannot cause an eleventh question. Any remaining gaps at the cap must be visible and constrain guidance. This precise rule supersedes the earlier answered-prompt wording.
IMPLEMENTATION SPECIFICATION
M04 adaptive intake specification
Owner: apps/api/app/modules/intake/service.py. Requirements R11 to R14. Inputs: current case, confirmed facts, existing question slots, approved pathway and urgency result. Output: a Question or review_required with unresolved gaps. If no eligible template remains, return review_required with the missing fields explicitly listed. No provider information enters question selection.
Deterministic selection with controlled AI wording
1. Reject unsafe state, stale revision or unsupported pathway. 2. Return the existing active prompt if one exists. 3. Stop at ten issued slots. 4. Calculate missing required fact keys and unresolved contradictions. 5. Filter approved templates by pathway eligibility and facts already known. 6. Order eligible templates by reviewed urgency priority, then the number of required missing keys they address, then template ID. 7. Issue the top template and increment the slot counter atomically.
Use reviewed question text for the first release. If optional local paraphrasing is enabled later, the question identity, choices, clinical meaning and required fields cannot change, and validated template wording is the fallback. A generic LLM must not generate arbitrary questions or change the count. Prioritize uncertainty that can change the next action rather than collecting an exhaustive history.
Answer validation
Type | Rules
single_choice | Exactly one issued option ID. The choice list includes unknown where clinically appropriate.
multiple_choice | Only issued option IDs; mutually exclusive none/unknown options cannot coexist with positive selections.
text | Trim outer whitespace; 1 to 2000 characters for answered. Preserve the original text and label any extracted interpretation.
scale | Integer within issued bounds; show verbal anchors. A missing value is not zero.
duration | Nonnegative number and unit in {hours,days,weeks,months,years}; preserve original unit.
unknown or skipped | No inferred answer value. Missing critical information remains an unresolved gap.
Stopping and clarification
Stop early only when reviewed required fields and pathway conditions are satisfied. Contradiction clarification consumes a new slot unless it is a direct edit of an existing answer. At the cap, generate only a limited result when necessary facts remain unknown. The screen says “Question n of up to 10”; optional profile, record review and doctor filters are clearly outside intake. New symptoms after completion create an edit or new case, not an unrestricted advice chat.

Run urgency and scope checks after the initial concern, each answer, clinical edit and confirmed document fact, and before displaying generated guidance. M06 record processing specification
Owner: modules/records/. Requirements R18 to R20. The first demo supports PDF documents and PNG/JPEG images of records. It extracts text and values; it does not diagnose from radiology images or symptom photographs.
Bounded processing pipeline
Verify file signature, declared media type, byte limit and page count. Reject encrypted or malformed PDFs and unsupported formats with a plain explanation. Store under a generated opaque key, never a user-provided path. Process in a restricted local worker without network access, shell execution or active content. Apply a 60-second processing timeout and a 40-megapixel decoded-image cap. Strip image metadata from derived previews.
Prefer native PDF text extraction; use local OCR when necessary. Extract structured facts with page locators and original excerpts. Preserve original units and reference intervals. Patient review
A04 lists processing states and shows extracted facts with Confirm, Correct and Reject actions. Unconfirmed facts may appear in the review interface but cannot support clinical conclusions. Confirming or correcting a fact increments the case revision and triggers urgency checks. Rejecting a fact excludes it while preserving the source only for the session’s permitted lifetime.
Injection and failure behavior
Document text is untrusted evidence. Instructions embedded in a record cannot modify system roles, call tools or change the pathway. The model adapter supplies extraction tasks with fixed schemas and no external capabilities. Log parser error codes without filenames or text. If extraction fails, offer a replacement file, manual facts or continuation without records. Do not fabricate a summary of an unreadable upload.
Acceptance examples
R18: an oversized image is rejected before decode; a file disguised with a PDF extension fails signature validation. R19: a lab value with a missing unit stays unit_unknown and retains its page. R20: an unconfirmed extracted allergy is not treated as confirmed history. An edit during extraction makes the pending output stale; deletion cancels processing and purges bytes.

M07 evidence and retrieval specification
Owner: modules/evidence/. Requirements R21 to R23. The approved local corpus is the primary source of clinical evidence. Provider lookup uses M09 adapters; M07 owns the shared outbound gateway policy and reference retrieval.
Corpus ingestion and freshness
Each source manifest includes publisher, permitted-use notes, URL, topic mapping, source date, retrieval date, reviewer, review status, review expiration and content hash. Chunk content by semantic section, retaining heading and page locators. Store embeddings locally. Only enabled, in-scope and unexpired reviewed sources can support a clinical claim. Version the index atomically so one guidance job sees one corpus snapshot.
Retrieve at most eight candidate passages using local keyword and embedding search, then retain at most five relevant passages within a 6000-token evidence budget. Insufficient evidence produces a limited result.

AI implementation and change protocol
Required workflow for every change
1. Read spec/lock.json, the affected requirement clauses, screen contracts, object schemas and owner modules. Verify the document and repository baseline hashes. 2. State affected IDs and planned files before editing. 3. Inspect existing code and tests; extend the owner module rather than building a parallel implementation. 4. Implement the smallest complete change, including loading, error and stale states. 5. Run the affected acceptance and UI tests, contract validation and trace checks. 6. Update evidence and document the result against the same IDs.
The AI may choose internal helper names within the owning package, but must register new files. It may not change routes, clinical rules, endpoint contracts, retention, scope or ranking semantics without a specification change. If information is missing, record a named decision and implement only independent, unambiguous work. Do not invent credentials, clinical review, working hardware access or passing tests.

Definition of done
A requirement is implemented when the registered paths exist and the behavior is complete. It is verified when its relevant tests pass against the release artifacts. A customer demo is ready only when every mandatory requirement is verified, no blocking defect remains, all screen routes work, and the presenter can accurately distinguish measured facts from planned outcomes. Do not mark a requirement done based on an AI statement or code generation alone.
